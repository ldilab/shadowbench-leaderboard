Compiling is not the same as being right

Compile rate counts a formalization as correct when its Lean code type-checks. ShadowBench's checkers test whether the statement actually says what the problem says. Here the two are compared on every output: 33 systems × 178 problems.

How this is counted
Compiles
The standalone Lean compile passes (a sorry is rejected), as in the leaderboard's Compile rate.
Aligned
Every forward and backward checker passes, whether or not the standalone compile did. Against the paper's expert review of compile-passed outputs, the checkers reach precision 1.000 and recall 0.930: they never accept a misaligned statement, and miss 3 of the 43 aligned ones (auxiliary declarations named differently from the reference).
False positive
Compiles but fails at least one checker: compile rate counts it, SA-PASS does not.
False negative
Passes every checker but does not compile, usually a proof left as sorry or a broken proof under a correct statement. Outputs that only hit the evaluator's compile time limit are shown separately.
Source
Per-problem evaluator records for the 33 leaderboard rows that have them (data/compile_confusion.json; the post-paper agentic runs do not yet); the counts reproduce every row's published Compile rate and SA-PASS.
